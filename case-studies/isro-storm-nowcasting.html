ISRO Storm Nowcasting

Project summary

Weather intelligence had to arrive as a decision, not a data dump.

From 2021 to 2024, I led weather-monitoring work at ISRO's National Atmospheric Research Laboratory. A central program was the adoption and operational use of a storm nowcasting system for short-horizon guidance. The work connected scientific modeling with the procedures, communication, and stakeholder confidence required in time-sensitive reviews.

My responsibility was program-level: align technical contributors, establish a reliable flow of evidence, evaluate performance, translate findings into operational language, and help decision makers understand both the signal and its limitations. The system supported four launch director reviews and extended the useful warning window from approximately 15 minutes toward two hours.

Challenge

Move faster without overstating certainty.

Convective weather can develop quickly, while launch decisions require disciplined review across multiple stakeholders. A model can be scientifically promising and still fail operationally if its outputs arrive too late, are difficult to interpret, or produce enough false alarms to erode trust.

The program therefore had two linked goals: improve short-horizon awareness and make the output usable in a senior operational review. Accuracy was necessary, but so were false-alarm behavior, lead time, escalation logic, and concise communication.

Constraints

Safety, time, and multi-source evidence.

  • Time pressure: The value of a weather signal falls quickly if review, interpretation, or escalation is slow.
  • False alarms: Over-warning can be operationally costly and can reduce confidence in future guidance.
  • Cross-functional interpretation: Scientists, engineers, and mission stakeholders needed a shared understanding of what the system could support.
  • Public communication limits: Operational details and internal artifacts are not reproduced here; the conceptual illustration contains no mission data or internal interface.

What I led

The operating layer around the model.

I spearheaded adoption of the nowcasting approach and coordinated the contributors responsible for observations, analysis, and operational communication. I helped define how outputs would be reviewed, which performance measures mattered, and how guidance should be framed for senior stakeholders.

I also connected this work to a larger coalition-building effort. By aligning 20 organizations around shared infrastructure and data governance, I secured $1M in government funding. That required resolving institutional interests, clarifying responsibilities, and demonstrating why a common capability would create more value than isolated efforts.

The program-management problem was to make scientific capability dependable at the exact moment another team needed to trust it.

Outcomes

Measured performance and operational adoption.

  • The evaluated nowcasting system reached 97.5% accuracy with a 5% false alarm rate.
  • Useful warning horizons ranged from 15 minutes to two hours.
  • The work supported four launch director reviews.
  • A coalition of 20 organizations enabled $1M in program funding and a shared governance model.

These are program and system outcomes. My contribution was leading adoption, alignment, evaluation, operating procedures, and stakeholder communication around the capability.

Methods

How the program was made operational.

  • Performance evaluation using accuracy, false-alarm behavior, and lead-time windows.
  • Cross-functional review structures connecting scientific and operational stakeholders.
  • Clear escalation and communication paths for time-sensitive guidance.
  • Coalition building, funding narratives, responsibility mapping, and data-governance design.
  • Iteration based on operational review rather than laboratory performance alone.

Reflection

What I would strengthen now.

I would make forecast provenance and uncertainty even more visible in the review surface, with structured confidence bands, signal freshness, and post-event calibration. I would also formalize decision logs linking guidance to observed outcomes so the organization could learn systematically from both correct calls and near misses.

The lasting lesson was that technical performance does not create trust by itself. Trust comes from repeatable operating behavior, explicit limits, and the quality of the conversations around the evidence.
