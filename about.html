About

I learned to make complexity useful.

My path runs from atmospheric science and field instruments to launch decisions, business strategy, and AI products. Each chapter added a different way of asking the same question: what does a team need to know, trust, and do next?

The through-line

Curiosity, translated into operating systems.

I am most engaged when a problem refuses to stay inside one discipline. At ISRO, weather models only mattered if scientists, engineers, vendors, and mission leaders could act on them together. At Simon, a sound recommendation also had to survive questions about economics, adoption, incentives, and execution.

That mix shaped how I work today. I am technical enough to interrogate the evidence, commercially aware enough to connect it to value, and comfortable coordinating the people who turn a recommendation into a program.

2013-2017

IIST

The Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology gave me a rigorous base in quantitative reasoning and systems thinking. Atmospheric behavior is a useful teacher: the system is dynamic, the observations are imperfect, and the model is always an approximation. I learned to respect assumptions, validate evidence, and stay curious when the first explanation was too easy.

That foundation led directly to work in atmospheric water vapor, GNSS observations, and remote sensing.

2017-2024

ISRO

At the National Atmospheric Research Laboratory, science became operational. I helped build a 25-station monitoring network, led weather-intelligence programs, coordinated institutional partners, and supported time-sensitive launch reviews. The work taught me that program management is not administrative overhead. It is the discipline that lets specialized people, fragile infrastructure, budgets, and consequential decisions function as one system.

It also made translation central to my identity: from instrument to dataset, model to guidance, and technical risk to stakeholder action.

2024-2026

Simon

I completed an MBA in Strategy and Technology to widen the lens. Finance, markets, analytics, and commercialization gave me better language for choices that technical teams already confront: where to invest, which customer to serve, what tradeoff is acceptable, and how to make adoption likely.

As VP of Marketing for the Data Analytics Club and VP of Finance for Net Impact, I practiced leadership without hierarchy. Volunteer teams sharpened my approach to clarity, motivation, and follow-through. Consulting and project work then applied those lessons to energy, sustainability, life sciences, and financial analytics.

Now

Building in public

RisQ, Nora, HELOC explainability, and the atmospheric research repositories make my process inspectable. They show how I frame a decision, structure evidence, work with code, and communicate limitations. Claude Code and Codex are part of that stack, but the goal is not automation for its own sake. It is to build tools that earn trust through useful behavior and clear boundaries.

People and perspective

Leadership is also a way of paying attention.

Teams, travel, sustainability, and outdoor experiments keep me alert to context. They make communication more human and remind me that confidence grows by engaging with the unfamiliar.

Contact

Let us compare notes on a difficult problem.

I am especially interested in teams where technical depth, stakeholder alignment, and business judgment have to coexist.
